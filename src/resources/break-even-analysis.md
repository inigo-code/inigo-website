## What break-even analysis answers

**Break-even analysis**, part of **cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis**, tells a manager how many units must be sold to cover all costs, how profit changes with volume, and how much sales can fall before the business makes a loss. It rests on one split: **fixed costs** (rent, salaries, insurance), which stay the same whatever the output, and **variable costs** (materials, direct labour, packing), which rise with every unit made.

## The formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | Selling price − Variable cost per unit |
| Total contribution | Sales − Total variable cost = Fixed cost + Profit |
| P/V ratio | Contribution ÷ Sales × 100 |
| Break-even point (units) | Fixed cost ÷ Contribution per unit |
| Break-even sales (₹) | Fixed cost ÷ P/V ratio |
| Sales for a target profit (units) | (Fixed cost + Target profit) ÷ Contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety | Actual sales − Break-even sales |
| Margin of safety ratio | Margin of safety ÷ Actual sales × 100 |
| Profit | Margin of safety × P/V ratio |

## Worked example

A small unit in Tiruchirappalli makes school bags.

- Selling price: ₹500 a bag
- Variable cost: ₹300 a bag
- Fixed cost: ₹2,00,000 a month
- Actual sales this month: 1,500 bags

**1. Contribution per unit** = 500 − 300 = **₹200**. Every bag sold adds ₹200 towards fixed cost, and once fixed cost is covered, ₹200 to profit.

**2. P/V ratio** = 200 ÷ 500 × 100 = **40%**.

**3. Break-even point** = 2,00,000 ÷ 200 = **1,000 bags**. In rupees: 2,00,000 ÷ 40% = **₹5,00,000** (check: 1,000 × ₹500).

**4. Sales needed for a profit of ₹60,000** = (2,00,000 + 60,000) ÷ 200 = **1,300 bags**, or ₹6,50,000.

**5. Profit at actual sales** of 1,500 bags = 1,500 × 200 − 2,00,000 = **₹1,00,000**.

**6. Margin of safety** = 1,500 − 1,000 = **500 bags**, or ₹7,50,000 − ₹5,00,000 = **₹2,50,000**. As a ratio: 2,50,000 ÷ 7,50,000 = **33.3%**. Sales can fall by a third before the unit makes a loss.

**Check:** profit = margin of safety × P/V ratio = 2,50,000 × 40% = ₹1,00,000. ✓

## Reading the break-even chart

The figure above is drawn to scale from these numbers.

- The **fixed cost line** is flat at ₹2 lakh.
- The **total cost line** starts at ₹2 lakh (the fixed cost) and rises by ₹300 for every bag.
- The **sales revenue line** starts at zero and rises by ₹500 for every bag.
- Where revenue crosses total cost is the **break-even point**. To the left is the loss area, to the right the profit area.
- The horizontal gap between actual sales and the break-even point is the **margin of safety**.

## What changes the break-even point

| Change | Effect on break-even point |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost rises | Rises |
| Selling price rises | Falls (contribution per unit rises) |
| Variable cost per unit rises | Rises (contribution per unit falls) |
| Sales volume rises | No change; margin of safety rises |

Try it with the example: if rent goes up so that fixed cost becomes ₹2,40,000, break-even rises to 1,200 bags. If the price rises to ₹550, contribution becomes ₹250 and break-even falls to 800 bags.

## Uses and limitations

**Uses:** setting sales targets, pricing decisions, deciding whether to make or buy, judging the risk of a new product or branch, and planning how far costs must fall to survive a slump.

**Limitations:** costs are rarely perfectly fixed or perfectly variable; selling prices often fall with volume (discounts); a multi-product firm's break-even depends on its sales mix; and the analysis is static, a picture of one period.

## Practice questions

1. Explain break-even analysis and its assumptions. Draw a break-even chart.
2. Fixed cost ₹1,50,000; selling price ₹60 a unit; variable cost ₹35 a unit. Find the P/V ratio, break-even point in units and rupees, and the sales needed for a profit of ₹50,000. *(Answer: 41.67%; 6,000 units, ₹3,60,000; 8,000 units.)*
3. Sales ₹4,00,000, profit ₹40,000, P/V ratio 25%. Find the fixed cost, break-even sales and margin of safety. *(Answer: ₹60,000; ₹2,40,000; ₹1,60,000.)*
4. What is the margin of safety? How can a firm improve it?
